Module 1

Signal classification and time domain representation

Definition

A signal is an electromagnetic or electrical representation of data. Signals are divided into Analog and Digital signals.

Time domain representation

A time-domain plot shows the instantaneous signal voltage (v) on the vertical axis against time (t) on the horizontal axis.

SignalTime-domain description
AnalogA continuous waveform whose amplitude changes smoothly over time, with infinitely many possible values.
DigitalA discrete square waveform switching between fixed voltage states, such as 0 V and 5 V.

Why analog vs. why digital

ProsCons
AnalogBest suited for natural physical phenomena (voice, radio waves) and uses less bandwidth.Suffers from cumulative noise and attenuation.
DigitalHigh noise immunity, easy regeneration via repeaters, and direct compatibility with binary hardware.Requires greater channel bandwidth.

Module 2

Periodic sinusoidal signals and core parameters

Definition

A periodic sinusoidal signal is a continuous signal that repeats a complete pattern over a fixed cycle called its time period (T).

Time period and frequency

QuantityMeaning
Time Period (T)The time, in seconds, required to complete one full cycle.
Frequency (f)The number of complete cycles per second, in Hertz (Hz).

f = 1 / T      T = 1 / f

EXAM PRACTICE BOX: FIND f AND T FROM A GRAPH

Question. The labeled sine wave below has a period marked as 20 ms. Find its time period in seconds and its frequency.

Read T from the graph: T = 20 ms = 20 × 10-3 s = 0.02 s
Apply f = 1 / T: f = 1 / 0.02 s = 50 Hz

Mathematical equations

v(t) = A sin(ωt)
v(t) = Vm sin(2πft + φ)

The three core parameters

ParameterDefinition
1. Amplitude
(Vm or A)		The peak vertical height (maximum voltage, e.g., 10 V) of the signal measured from the horizontal axis.
2. Frequency (f)The rate of oscillation per second.
3. Phase
(φ or θ)		The position of a waveform relative to time t = 0, measured in degrees or radians.

Phase shift cases and the unit circle

The four standard phase shifts correspond to the four axis points of the unit circle: 0°, 90°, 180°, and 270°.

PhaseEquationBehavior at t = 0
0°v = 10 sin(2πft + 0°)Starts at 0 V and rises toward its peak at 90°, is zero at 180°, and reaches its trough at 270°.
90°v = Vm sin(2πft + 90°)Shifted left by 1/4 cycle, so it starts at its maximum positive amplitude (Vm).
180°v = Vm sin(2πft + 180°)Shifted by 1/2 cycle, so it starts at 0 V and immediately goes negative.
270°v = Vm sin(2πft + 270°)Shifted by 3/4 cycle, so it starts at its negative peak (trough).

Module 3

Frequency domain representation and unit conversions

Definition

The frequency domain plots peak amplitude (V) on the vertical axis against frequency (f, in Hz) on the horizontal axis. An entire periodic sine wave is represented by a single vertical spike.

Lecture examples

  • 6 Hz sine wave. A time-domain sine wave with peak amplitude 5 V completes 6 cycles inside 1 s (6 Hz). It maps to one spike of height 5 V at 6 Hz on an axis numbered 1 to 8 Hz.
  • 0 Hz (DC) signal. A flat line at 10 V over time t is a constant voltage that never oscillates, so it corresponds to 0 Hz.

Time domain vs. frequency domain

AdvantageDisadvantage
Time DomainShows the instantaneous voltage at any time t.Gets cluttered for multi-tone signals.
Frequency DomainCleanly isolates component frequencies and bandwidth.Hides instantaneous time behavior.

Module 4

Composite signals, bandwidth and a worked problem

Definition

A composite signal is a signal made of multiple sinusoidal signals combined together.

Real-world examples are human speech and music. A single-frequency sine wave is not useful for carrying real information, so practical signals are composites.

Superposition:

S = S1 + S2 + S3
= A1 sin(2πf1t) + A2 sin(2πf2t) + A3 sin(2πf3t)

Bandwidth of a composite signal

Definition

By default, analog bandwidth refers to a composite signal and measures the width of the frequency range it occupies.

Bandwidth (BW) = fH - fL   (Hz)

Here fH is the highest frequency component and fL is the lowest frequency component.

Worked 3-part lecture problem

PROMPT

If a periodic analog signal is decomposed into 5 sine waves with frequencies of 100, 300, 500, 700, 900 Hz: (i) What is the bandwidth? (ii) Draw the frequency domain representation assuming all components have a maximum amplitude of 10 V. (iii) Write the equations of these signals assuming they have the same phase.

Solution (i): Bandwidth

BW = fH - fL
= 900 - 100
= 800 Hz

Solution (ii): Frequency domain plot

Solution (iii): Signal equations

Assuming zero phase (φ = 0):

S1 = 10 sin(2π · 100 t)
S2 = 10 sin(2π · 300 t)
S3 = 10 sin(2π · 500 t)
S4 = 10 sin(2π · 700 t)
S5 = 10 sin(2π · 900 t)

S(t) = S1 + S2 + S3 + S4 + S5

Module 5

Multi-level digital signals

Definition

A digital signal with L discrete voltage levels can encode log2(L) bits in each signal element (signal level).

Bits per level = log2(L)

SignalLevel mappingCapacity
2-LevelLevel 1 (0 V) represents binary 0 and Level 2 (5 V) represents binary 1.log2(2) = 1 bit per signal level
4-LevelFour discrete voltage levels (Level 1 to Level 4) represent the 2-bit pairs 00, 01, 10, 11.log2(4) = 2 bits per signal level, doubling the data rate for the same symbol rate

Advantages and disadvantages

AdvantagesDisadvantages
2-LevelSimple hardware, large voltage gap between levels, high noise immunity.Lower bit rate.
4-LevelDoubles the bit rate per baud.Narrower voltage gaps increase susceptibility to noise and bit errors.
